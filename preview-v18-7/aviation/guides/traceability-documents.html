Aviation procurement guide

CoC, FAA 8130-3, EASA Form 1 & Traceability

What documentation requests should distinguish before a sourcing offer is accepted.

All buyer guides ↗

Start with part identity

Match the Part Number, Serial Number and quantity on all applicable records to the physical article. Mismatches or incomplete traceability should be investigated rather than explained away in marketing copy.

Certificate of Conformity

A CoC is a supplier/manufacturer conformity statement whose value depends on its issuer, scope and applicable standards. It is not automatically equivalent to an authorized release certificate.

Authorized release documents

FAA Form 8130-3 and EASA Form 1 are official record forms used in defined contexts. Their issuance and acceptability depend on the specific process and jurisdiction. Do not treat either form as universally mandatory or automatically sufficient.

Airline trace

An airline traceability request may require records linking a component to an airline/operator or maintenance history. Define which chain of evidence is needed, rather than asking only for a phrase on a quote.

Calibration and test reports

Tools and measuring equipment may require calibration certificates; repairable components may have test or maintenance records. Verify the certificate scope, date, identity and issuing party.

Procurement gate

Ask suppliers to provide sample document descriptions where practical before an order. Technical, quality and trade-compliance approval must be independent of the sales presentation.
