Aircraft Part Number RFQ: Buyer Checklist

How to describe an aviation spare-part request so suppliers can return comparable offers.

All buyer guides ↗

Begin with identification

Use the complete Part Number (P/N) exactly as marked or listed in an approved source. Preserve hyphens, letters, suffixes and dash numbers. State the manufacturer and equipment designation independently if they are known; a number without its issuing context may be ambiguous.

Describe the intended application

Provide the aircraft or equipment family and configuration information where appropriate. Include any verified IPC, maintenance-manual or drawing references. Do not treat a model-name match as proof of installation eligibility.

Specify quantity and condition

State the number of units, unit of measure and acceptable conditions. If you can evaluate new and serviceable units, request separate quotes and document sets. Avoid combining different conditions into one comparison line.

Define the evidence package

Indicate whether a CoC, release certificate, traceability, test record or airline trace is required. The correct documents depend on part category, jurisdiction and end-user requirements; do not demand an irrelevant certificate as a universal rule.

Add commercial constraints

Give destination, delivery deadline, preferred shipping basis and response contact. Clarify what is required for export-control review, including end user and intended use when applicable.

Compare offers on identical terms

Normalize quotation currency, condition, quantity, stock evidence, lead time, document availability, warranty and logistics assumptions. Reject vague claims of inventory or certification without supporting evidence.
