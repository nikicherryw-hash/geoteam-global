New, Serviceable, Overhauled: Aviation Parts Conditions

Practical distinctions buyers should clarify when requesting aircraft parts.

All buyer guides ↗

Condition is not one universal code

Market labels such as NE, NS, SV, OH, AR and AS-IS can vary in their commercial use. Ask each supplier to define its condition notation and provide the supporting record package, rather than assuming a label is a regulatory determination.

New parts

For new items, identify whether the product is manufacturer new, new surplus or another seller-defined category. Ask for packaging status, batch traceability, shelf-life information where relevant and the applicable conformity/release documents.

Serviceable units

A serviceable unit should have specific evidence supporting its offered status. Check whether the supplied documentation matches the actual part and serial number, and whether the record type is acceptable for the intended operation.

Overhauled and repaired units

Overhauled, repaired and inspected equipment are not interchangeable terms. Record the scope of work, work-performing organization, relevant test results and certificate or release documentation where applicable.

As removed and as is

An as-removed component may require further inspection, repair or testing. Treat it as a distinct procurement scenario, not a lower-priced alternative to a serviceable unit without a clear technical review.

Acceptance decision

A supplier description does not itself confer airworthiness. Authorized maintenance and quality personnel determine the documentation and release status required by the operator and regulator.
